The first law of thermodynamics expresses conservation of energy during a thermodynamic process. Begin by choosing the system, stating the sign convention and identifying heat and work.

## What you will learn

- Apply the sign convention commonly used in chemistry.
- Calculate internal-energy changes using the first law.
- Explain enthalpy and the conditions under which constant-pressure heat equals the enthalpy change.

## 1. Energy entering and leaving the system

For a closed system, neglecting changes in its overall kinetic and potential energies:

$$
\Delta U = q + w
$$

This course uses the following convention:

| Energy transfer | Sign |
| --- | --- |
| Heat enters the system | $q>0$ |
| Heat leaves the system | $q<0$ |
| Work is done on the system | $w>0$ |
| Work is done by the system | $w<0$ |

For pressure–volume work against a constant external pressure:

$$
w = -P_{\mathrm{ext}}\Delta V
$$

During expansion, $\Delta V>0$. Energy passes from the system to the surroundings, so $w<0$. If the external pressure varies, use $w=-\int P_{\mathrm{ext}}\,\mathrm dV$.

## 2. Worked example: heat absorbed while doing work

A closed system absorbs $250\ \mathrm J$ of heat and does $80\ \mathrm J$ of work on its surroundings.

- Heat absorbed: $q=+250\ \mathrm J$.
- Work done by the system: $w=-80\ \mathrm J$.

$$
\Delta U = 250 + (-80) = +170\ \mathrm J
$$

The internal energy increases by $170\ \mathrm J$. A frequent error is to assign a positive sign to work done by the system.

## 3. Why introduce enthalpy?

**Enthalpy** is defined by:

$$
H = U + PV
$$

At constant pressure:

$$
\Delta H = \Delta U + P\Delta V
$$

For a **closed system at constant pressure with only pressure–volume work**, $w=-P\Delta V$. Substitution into the first law gives:

$$
q_P = \Delta H
$$

Keep the conditions explicit. If electrical or other non-expansion work occurs, the total constant-pressure heat cannot simply be identified with the enthalpy change.

## 4. Constant volume and constant pressure

In a rigid container, $\Delta V=0$. If pressure–volume work is the only possible work, $w=0$ and:

$$
q_V=\Delta U
$$

Constant-volume heat then measures the internal-energy change. Constant-pressure heat under the conditions above measures the enthalpy change. Both are changes in state functions, but heat itself remains path dependent.

## Check your understanding

1. Is pressure–volume work positive or negative when a gas is compressed?
2. A system loses $100\ \mathrm J$ of heat while $40\ \mathrm J$ of work is done on it. Find $\Delta U$.
3. State the conditions needed to use $q_P=\Delta H$.

**Answers:** Positive; $-60\ \mathrm J$; a closed system at constant pressure with only pressure–volume work, neglecting overall kinetic- and potential-energy changes.
